Getting Jodi onto the App Store

A practical build-and-launch plan for a South Asian dating app — what exists today, what has to be built, what Apple will demand, and roughly what it costs.

1. Where you actually are

You have a complete, interactive product design: the curated five-a-day deck, prompt-based profiles, compatibility scoring, voice intros, the crushes grid, chat, the match moment, and a verified profile screen. That is genuinely valuable — it is the artefact you hand a developer, an investor, or a designer, and it removes months of ambiguity.

What you do not have is a working app. There is no database, no accounts, no real messaging, no photo storage, no matching engine, and no iOS binary. Everything in the prototype is simulated in the browser.

The honest framing: the design is roughly 10–15% of the work of shipping a dating app. The other 85% is backend engineering, trust & safety, and solving the cold-start problem — getting enough real people in one city that the app doesn't feel empty.

3. What has to be built

Core

  • Phone-number or Apple/Google sign-in, with an 18+ date-of-birth gate
  • Profile creation: photo upload with compression, prompts, voice-intro recording
  • Matching engine — the daily-five curation logic and the compatibility score
  • Like / pass / rose actions with a daily quota
  • Realtime chat with push notifications
  • Payments for Jodi Gold (see the Apple rules below — this one has a trap in it)

Trust & safety — not optional, and Apple checks it

  • Photo verification: a selfie-pose check matched against profile photos. Buy this — Veriff, Persona, or Onfido — do not build it.
  • Report and block on every profile and every conversation
  • A moderation queue and a real human who reads it
  • Automated screening of photos and messages for nudity and abuse
  • A published way for users to contact you, and a commitment to act on reports within 24 hours

The UNiDAYS idea: student verification is a nice trust signal and a good growth wedge, but it is a partnership you have to apply for, and it cannot substitute for real ID or photo verification. Treat it as a badge, not as your safety system.

4. Legal groundwork

Do this before you write the app, not after. A lawyer for a few hours is far cheaper than a rewrite.

  • Form a company (an LLC is typical in the US) so liability doesn't sit on you personally
  • Terms of Service and a Privacy Policy, both publicly hosted — Apple requires working links
  • GDPR / CCPA compliance if you have users in Europe or California: data export, data deletion, consent
  • In-app account deletion — Apple mandates this for any app with account creation
  • Age-verification and safety language written into your terms
  • Trademark search on the name Jodi before you spend anything on branding

5. The App Store, specifically

Set-up

  • Join the Apple Developer Program — $99/year. As a company you'll need a D-U-N-S number, which takes 1–2 weeks, so start it early.
  • You need a Mac and Xcode to build and upload.
  • Create the app record in App Store Connect, then ship builds to testers through TestFlight.

Listing assets you'll need

  • App icon at 1024×1024, plus screenshots for 6.7" and 6.5" iPhones
  • Name (30 characters), subtitle (30), description, and keywords (100)
  • An 17+ or 18+ age rating, set honestly
  • Support URL and marketing URL

Budget 1–3 days for the first review, and expect at least one rejection. It is normal. Fix and resubmit.

6. The hard part: cold start

A dating app with a hundred users in the wrong city is worthless. This kills far more dating apps than bad code does.

  • Launch in exactly one city. For a South Asian app: Jersey City / Edison, Toronto (Brampton or Mississauga), the Bay Area, or London. Pick one.
  • Aim for roughly 500–1,000 real, active users in that city before you go public. Below that, the daily five will be padded with people who aren't a fit — and your entire premise collapses.
  • Seed through South Asian student associations, garba and Eid events, temple and mosque youth groups, and desi creators on Instagram and TikTok.
  • Recruit women first. Every dating app skews male; the women-initiate rule helps, but only if the women are there.
  • Run a waitlist by city and open each one only when it's dense enough.

7. A realistic sequence

Months 0–1 — validate

  • Show this prototype to 30–50 people in your target group. Watch them use it. Get a waitlist going. Trademark search and company formation.

Months 1–2 — get a builder

  • Choose your path from section 2. Lock the feature list for v1 — resist adding to it. Get the legal documents drafted.

Months 2–6 — build

  • Backend, apps, verification and moderation. Apple Developer account and D-U-N-S in parallel. TestFlight with 50–100 real users from the waitlist.

Month 6 — submit

  • Listing assets, privacy labels, demo account, review notes explaining your differentiation. Submit; expect a rejection; resubmit.

Months 6–9 — one city

  • Open your single city. Events and creator partnerships. Watch match rate, reply rate, and day-7 retention. Only expand when the first city holds.

Ongoing costs to plan for: Apple $99/yr · backend $25–300/mo early on · photo verification roughly $0.50–2.00 per user · push notifications and SMS a few hundred a month at small scale · plus a real person's time on moderation from day one.

8. What I can do next

  • Design the missing flows — onboarding, profile creation, photo and ID verification, the paywall, and reporting. A developer will need all of them, and they don't exist yet.
  • Produce a developer handoff package — the design plus specifications an engineer can quote and build from.
  • Build the App Store listing assets — screenshots, icon, and the description copy.
  • Write the spec for the matching algorithm — how the compatibility score is actually computed from the values questions.
